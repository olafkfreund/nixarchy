# Intent: No check or script fails because grep found what it was looking for

## Problem

#1058 proved a mechanism.

- `producer | grep -q pattern` under `pipefail` fails when grep matches early
  and exits before the producer's next `write()`. That write takes SIGPIPE, so
  `PIPESTATUS=141 0`: grep said yes, and the pipeline said no.
- It needs the producer preempted between writes, so it passes thousands of
  times locally and fails intermittently on busy CI runners.
- `checks.manifest-has-kind` failed that way once, and #1061 fixed that one
  site.

The same shape appears about a hundred times in this tree, in 34 files
(listed in #1060): tests, installer scripts, `pkgs/verify.sh`, workflows.
Some sites cannot fail:

- no `pipefail`;
- a producer that writes once, such as a single `echo`;
- a match that can only be on the last line.

Some can. Nobody knows which, and a false red teaches people to re-run reds.
The failures that are not in checks are worse. A false "not found" in
`installer/install.sh` or `pkgs/verify.sh` is a wrong answer given to a user
on real hardware.

## Proposed outcome

Every `… | grep -q` in the tree is either rewritten to a form that cannot
fail on a match, or left alone with a one-line reason why it cannot. The
distinction comes from reading each site, not from assuming. A new occurrence
of the exposed shape is caught mechanically rather than by review.

## Affected users and systems

The 34 files in #1060:

- `tests/*.nix`, including the VM tests' `machine.succeed` strings;
- `installer/install.sh`, `pkgs/verify.sh`, `pkgs/*.nix` and `modules/apps.nix`;
- `.github/workflows/*.yml`, where GitHub's default `bash` shell runs with
  `-eo pipefail`.

Plus `tests/AGENTS.md`, which already carries the mechanism
(`#a-pipe-into-grep-q-can-fail-because-grep-matched`).

## Constraints

- **Each rewritten site keeps its meaning.** Same pattern, same polarity
  (`!` and `||` included), same failure message.
- **Workflow files are CI-gate territory (§11).** Editing the shell inside a
  step changes no trigger or required check, but it is still flagged to a
  human in the PR.
- **VM-test strings are Python strings.** A rewrite must survive the driver's
  own quoting, and the test driver lint.
- **§1 per class, not per site.** Proving a hundred sites fail one by one
  would mean breaking a hundred things. The spec decides which
  representatives are proven, and how the mechanical guard is itself proven
  to fail.

## Open questions

1. **A guard, or only the rewrite?** Default proposal: a cheap
   `checks.grep-q-pipefail` that greps the tree for `| grep -q` (and
   `grep -[a-z]*q`) outside an allowlist of reviewed-safe sites, each with its
   reason. That is the §4 "list that names things wants a comparison" answer:
   a new occurrence fails its own PR.
2. **Scope of rewriting.** Rewrite every site, including provably safe ones,
   for uniformity, or only the exposed ones, with safe ones allowlisted?
   Default proposal: rewrite every site where the rewrite is mechanical and
   obviously equivalent (capture, then `[[ == ]]`, or grep on a here-string),
   and allowlist only what cannot be rewritten simply. A smaller allowlist is
   a guard that says more.
