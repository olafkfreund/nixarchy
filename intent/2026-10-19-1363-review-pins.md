# Intent: the nightly review watches every package the daily bump updates

## Problem

The nightly review (`pkgs/review.sh`, reported in #1331) asks whether each
hand-pinned package is behind upstream. Its list of packages is hand-written,
at `pkgs/review.sh:114-123`, and names six: once, hey-cli, omacalc, omacut,
omawrite, ttfx.

The daily bump (`.#update`) updates every `pkgs/apps/*.nix` that has an
`updateScript`. That is eight files: those six plus **owe** and **aether**.

So the review is silent about two pinned packages. That matters now: #1359
carries a patch to owe until omacom/owe#18 ships, and the review is where an
owe release would be noticed. `tests/review-pins.nix` hardcodes the same six
names, on purpose (a list derived from the script would agree with it by
construction), so nothing compares the review's list with what the bump
actually updates. CLAUDE.md §4: a hand-maintained list fails open.

## Proposed outcome

- The review reports owe and aether like the other six.
- A check fails, naming the package, when a `pkgs/apps/*.nix` with an
  `updateScript` is missing from the review's list. A ninth pinned app then
  cannot be forgotten.
- `tests/review-pins.nix` keeps its independent hardcoded list, and gains the
  two names.

## Affected users and systems

- `pkgs/review.sh` (two rows), `tests/review-pins.nix` (two names, plus the
  comparison). Nothing a user installs changes.
- The nightly review's table gains two rows.

## Constraints

- §1: the comparison must fail when a row is removed, and name it.
- The comparison must not be derived from review.sh's own list. It reads the
  `pkgs/apps/*.nix` sources for `updateScript`, which is what `.#update`
  selects on.
- No network in the check (`--list-pins` mode, as today).

## Open questions

- **Read `updateScript` from source with grep, or from the flake's evaluated
  `nixarchy-apps`?** I propose grep over `pkgs/apps/*.nix`. It is cheap and
  runs inside a `runCommand`, and `.#update` selects on the same attribute.
  The flake evaluation is more exact, but it would make a seconds-long check
  evaluate nixpkgs.
